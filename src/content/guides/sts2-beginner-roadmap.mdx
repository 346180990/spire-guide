Everyone remembers their first Spire run. You pick some cards that look cool, die to an Act 1 elite, and wonder what you did wrong. This roadmap is for that moment — the ten things I wish someone had told me before my first ten hours with Slay the Spire 2.

## Step 1: Start with the Ironclad

The Ironclad is the most forgiving character for new players. His starting relic heals you after every fight, so you can make mistakes and recover. His game plan is straightforward — play big attacks, kill things, move on. Start here. Leave the Silent and Defect for when you understand enemy patterns.

## Step 2: Skip Cards More Than You Take Them

The single biggest mistake new players make: taking a card reward every single fight. Your deck gets worse when you add mediocre cards. A 35-card deck with 15 Strikes and Defends is a losing deck.

**Rule of thumb**: Skip card rewards at least 40% of the time. Ask yourself: "Does this card make my deck better right now?" If the answer isn't an immediate yes, skip it.

## Step 3: Remove Strikes Before Defends

At shops, always remove Strikes first. Strike deals 6 damage for 1 energy. Most character cards deal 8-14 damage for 1 energy, plus an effect. Every Strike in your hand is a card that could have been something better.

Defends stay relevant longer because block is block — 5 block for 1 energy is always useful. Remove Strikes first, then Defends once your block cards are solid.

## Step 4: Fight Elites (Seriously)

New players avoid elites because they're scary. Experienced players path toward them because relics win runs.

**Act 1 elite target**: 2-3 elites minimum. The Ironclad's sustain makes this safe. Take 2-3 strong attacks by floor 6, then hunt elites for relics.

**Act 2 elite target**: 1-2 elites if your deck is strong, 0 if you're struggling. Act 2 elites are the hardest in the game.

Relics are permanent power. Cards can be removed, but relics stack forever. Each elite you avoid is a relic you'll never get.

## Step 5: Learn to Evaluate Cards in Tiers

Not all cards are created equal. Here's a quick evaluation framework:

- **S-Tier (always pick, build around)**: Cards that define your win condition. Demon Form, Catalyst, Echo Form.
- **A-Tier (strong in most decks)**: Efficient damage, draw, or block. Shrug It Off, Dagger Throw, Cold Snap.
- **B-Tier (situationally good)**: Cards that need support to shine. Accuracy needs shivs, Feel No Pain needs exhaust.
- **C-Tier (rarely pick)**: Under-statted or over-costed. Clash, Setup, most colorless cards.
- **Skip-Tier (actively bad)**: Cards that make your deck worse. These exist!

## Step 6: Campfires — Upgrade Over Rest

Your goal at campfires: upgrade at least 70% of the time. Resting is a safety net for when things go wrong, not your plan A.

**Upgrade priority**: Win condition cards first (Demon Form), then key block cards, then card draw. Never upgrade Strikes or Defends unless you have a very specific reason.

## Step 7: Understand the Act 1 Boss Pool

The Act 1 boss determines a lot about your draft. After you see which boss is coming (floor 8 boss chest preview):

- **Slime Boss**: Front-loaded burst damage is essential. You need to split him before he overwhelms you.
- **The Guardian**: Thorn damage punishes multi-hit attacks. Take big single-hit cards instead. Block is important.
- **Hexaghost**: Scaling damage. His turn 2 attack deals damage based on your current HP. Don't heal to full before this fight — you're paying HP for nothing.

## Step 8: Path for Campfires, Then Elites, Then ?

Your path through each act should prioritize:
1. Campfires (for upgrades)
2. Elites (for relics)
3. Shops (for removals and key relics/cards)
4. Normal fights (for card rewards and gold)

Normal fights are the lowest-value floors. Take paths with more campfires and elites, even if it means fewer total floors.

## Step 9: Don't Force Archetypes

The game gives you random cards. You can't force Demon Form every run. Sometimes the game hands you three Anger cards and a Rampage — that's your signal. Play the deck you're given, not the one you theorycrafted.

The best Slay the Spire players win 30-40% of their runs on high ascension. That means they lose 60-70% of the time. Losses are data. Each death teaches you about a card you overvalued or an elite you weren't ready for.

## Step 10: Know When a Run Is Lost (and When It Isn't)

The Spire is about managing probability. A run where you're at 12 HP entering Act 2 with a mediocre deck is probably over — and that's okay. Restart and go again.

But runs where you're low HP with a strong deck core? Those are winnable. The Ironclad can heal to full from 5 HP if you path carefully and find a Reaper. Don't abandon runs that have good bones just because your HP is low.

## The First Win Checklist

When you're ready to close out your first run, make sure you have:
- [ ] 3-4 energy per turn (energy relic from boss chest is ideal)
- [ ] A clear win condition (Strength scaling, poison stack, orb engine)
- [ ] 15-25 cards (smaller is better — makes you draw your good cards more often)
- [ ] At least 1 AOE solution for multi-enemy fights
- [ ] Reliable block — either high-value block cards or a scaling defense engine

Good luck. The Spire's not going to slay itself.
