The Necrobinder is Slay the Spire 2's high-risk, high-reward specialist — a dark mage who weaponizes the very things every other character removes from their deck. Curses aren't a punishment; they're fuel. Minions aren't allies; they're resources to be sacrificed. The Necrobinder plays a different game entirely, and when her engine clicks, it generates power levels that make Demon Form look pedestrian. But misplay a single turn and you'll understand why necromancy is forbidden.

## Core Mechanics

The Necrobinder's starting relic, **Soul Vessel**, stores up to 3 souls (gained when any minion or enemy dies). Souls are a secondary resource used to power Binding effects. Unlike the Regent's Command which resets each combat, souls persist between turns but empty at the end of each fight.

Three interlocking mechanics define her:

1. **Curse Synergy** — Curse cards normally punish you with negative effects. The Necrobinder turns them into advantages. Curses in your hand grant bonuses to your other cards. The more Curses in your deck and hand, the stronger your payoffs become. It's not about removing Curses — it's about collecting them.

2. **Sacrifice** — Summon temporary minions, then sacrifice them for burst effects. A minion with 12 HP isn't there to attack — it's there to be killed on command for 12 AoE damage, or 12 block, or 12 souls. The calculus is cold: every minion is a grenade with a timer.

3. **Soul Binding** — Spend accumulated souls on Binding effects — powerful actions that scale with the number of souls spent. A Binding that deals 5 damage per soul with 3 souls is fine. The same Binding with 8 souls ends boss fights.

Three major archetypes:

1. **Soul Cycle** — Generate souls rapidly through minion sacrifice and enemy kills. Spend them on Binding payoffs. The engine archetype — ramp souls, spend souls, repeat. Most consistent and recommended for learning the character.
2. **Curse Weaver** — Add Curses to your deck intentionally. Play Curse-payoff cards that scale with the number of Curses in your hand. The deck gets worse on paper but stronger in practice with every Curse added.
3. **Minion Master** — Build an army of undead minions that persist and scale. Unlike Sacrifice builds that kill minions immediately, Minion Master invests in keeping them alive. Higher ceiling but vulnerable to AoE enemies.

## Starting Deck

The Necrobinder starts with:
- 4x **Strike** (deal 6 damage)
- 4x **Defend** (gain 5 block)
- 1x **Soul Drain** (deal 8 damage, gain 1 Soul if the enemy dies)
- 1x **Curse of Binding** (add a Binding Curse to your hand — Binding Curse is Ethereal, deals 3 damage when drawn)

Curse of Binding is your introduction to Curse synergy. The Binding Curse it creates costs 0, exhausts at end of turn, and deals 3 damage just for drawing it. That's not a downside — it's a free 3 damage. Upgrade Soul Drain first to gain 2 Souls on kill instead of 1.

## Best Relics

**Dark Grimoire** — The first Curse you draw each turn also draws 1 card. The Necrobinder's equivalent of Ring of the Snake — turns Curses into card advantage.

**Sacrificial Dagger** — When you sacrifice a minion, deal 8 damage to ALL enemies (instead of just the targeted enemy). Transforms single-target sacrifice into board clears.

**Soul Catalyst** — You can store up to 5 souls (instead of 3). More souls = bigger Binding payoffs = more dead bosses.

**Bone Amulet** — Minions have +5 max HP. Gives you more sacrifice value per minion AND makes Minion Master builds viable. High priority.

## Act-by-Act Strategy

### Act 1
Build the soul engine. Soul Drain and Spirit Leech are premium early picks — they deal damage AND generate souls. Take 1-2 Curses if offered (they'll feel bad immediately but pay off in Act 2). The Necrobinder's Act 1 is her weakest — she needs engine pieces before she can exploit them. Play conservatively.

### Act 2
The engine clicks. By now you should have reliable soul generation and 2-3 Binding payoffs. Curse Weaver builds come online with Malediction (deal 12 damage, +4 per Curse in hand) and Dark Pact (draw 3 cards, add a Curse to your discard pile). Soul Cycle builds need a way to spend souls every turn — Unbound Spirit and Soul Eruption are top priorities.

### Act 3
The power curve spikes. A Necrobinder in Act 3 with 5+ Curses in deck, 3+ soul capacity, and upgraded Binding payoffs can one-shot most hallway fights on turn 1. For bosses, stack souls in phase 1, then unleash a 10+ soul Binding finisher. The Awakened One is exceptionally easy — phase 2 counts as a new combat, resetting your soul count back to full for a second round.

## Common Mistakes

- **Removing Curses at shops**: This is the single most common Necrobinder error. You're not playing Ironclad. Curses are your synergy pieces. Removing them is like removing Strength cards from an Ironclad deck.
- **Sacrificing too early**: A minion at full HP that attacks for 3 turns is worth 3x its sacrifice value. Sacrifice is a finisher, not an opener — unless you have Sacrificial Dagger and can end the fight immediately.
- **Ignoring soul generation**: Without souls, Binding cards are dead draws. You need at least 3 sources of soul generation by mid-Act 2. Soul Drain alone isn't enough.
- **HP as resource mismanagement**: The Necrobinder uses HP more aggressively than any other character. 20 HP is comfortable. 10 HP is workable. 5 HP is concerning but you can still win. Play at the edge, but know where the edge is.

## Summary

The Necrobinder is not for beginners. She demands comfort with unconventional resource management (Curses as assets, souls as currency, HP as spending money) and punishes tactical errors more severely than any other character. But for players who master her, she offers the highest damage ceilings and most explosive turns in Slay the Spire 2. Start with Soul Cycle — it's the most forgiving archetype and teaches soul economy fundamentals. Move to Curse Weaver once you've broken the mental habit of removing Curses. Save Minion Master for when you can reliably predict enemy attack patterns.
